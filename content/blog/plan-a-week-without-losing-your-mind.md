Most week planners fail for the same reason: they ask you to decide seven dinners on
Sunday, when you have no idea what you will want on Thursday. So you skip it, order
in, and the plan dies.

## Plan the shape, not every dish

Instead of picking recipes for every slot, decide the **shape** of your week first:

- which nights you will actually cook, and which you will not
- how long you have each night (15 minutes, 30, an hour)
- one or two cuisines you feel like this week

Then let the planner fill the slots with recipes that fit the shape. You can swap any
of them before you shop.

## Cook once, eat twice

Build the week so each cooking session covers two meals. A big pot of ragù becomes
pasta tonight and a baked rigatoni later in the week. A roast chicken becomes dinner,
then sandwiches, then stock. You cook three or four times, not seven.

## Shop the list, not the recipe

When the week is set, send the combined ingredient list to your supermarket. You shop
once, you waste less, and you do not buy a jar of something you will use for one
recipe and never again.

## Save what worked

The meals that went well go into your own book. The next time you plan, the planner
leans on what you already enjoyed — not on a generic list someone else wrote.

That is the whole method. Shape, fill, cook once, shop once, keep what worked.
